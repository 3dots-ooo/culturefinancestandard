Culture creates value that finance still can’t see.

The Culture Finance Standard is a shared way to assess and verify that value, so funders can back cultural projects and report on them the way they already do for climate and social impact.

Why we’re building it

Climate has its standards.Social impact has its measurement.Cultural value is still the largest thing sustainability finance doesn’t measure.

Money follows what can be measured. As long as cultural value stays invisible in reporting, it stays out of investment decisions, however much it matters to people and places.

The standard brings together years of our work at 3dots on new ways to fund culture, with partners from finance, law and research.

Six areas where culture creates value

Every project is looked at across six cultural impact areas. Under them sit 141 indicators.

  • Participation and access
  • Livelihoods and creative economies
  • Place and heritage
  • Social cohesion and peacebuilding
  • Cognitive environment
  • Climate-linked cultural resilience

How a project is assessed

Safeguards come first. Seven safeguard gates screen out harm, including displacement, extraction, elite capture, cognitive harm and instrumentalisation. A project that fails a safeguard can’t be listed, whatever its other results.

The project is then assessed on depth, breadth and duration: how deep its effect goes, how widely it reaches and how long it lasts. That places it in one of four shades of impact.

  • SymbolicDoesn’t qualify on its own
  • Incremental
  • Structural
  • Transformative

Results are reported as contributions. In complex systems no single project causes change on its own, so none is asked to claim it.

The Gallery

The Gallery is the platform where the standard is used. It connects cultural organisations looking for funding with funders looking for verified cultural impact.

If you run a cultural project

You submit your project against the standard. You don’t need specialist knowledge, and listing is free.

Independent assessors review it and check every safeguard. Once it’s approved, a Cultural Contribution Unit (CCU) is recorded on its profile and the project appears in The Gallery, where funders anywhere can find it.

If you fund culture

You can browse projects by impact area, shade of impact or place. Every listing has passed the safeguards, and each one shows its full assessment.

When you fund a project and the contribution is confirmed, its CCU is added to your Impact Record. The record is audit-ready and produces disclosures in line with CSRD, SFDR and other sustainable finance frameworks.

A CCU is a record of a verified contribution. It pays no return and can’t be sold or transferred. The Gallery works with any kind of funding: grants, loans, equity, blended finance or sponsorship.
